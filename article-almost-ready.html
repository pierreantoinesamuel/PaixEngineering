MANUFACTURING / AUG 12, 2026 / 06 MIN READ

When “almost ready” is the most expensive phase.

A prototype can look finished long before the product is ready to repeat.

The first signal is unresolved variation. If every sample needs a different adjustment, the design or process has not yet found its stable center. The second is hidden labor: parts that technically assemble only because one experienced person knows exactly how to persuade them into place.

The third signal is a growing list of exceptions. A note that says “rework if needed” may seem harmless, but several exceptions usually point to a requirement that has not been designed into the product. The fourth is an inspection plan that checks dimensions without explaining which functions those dimensions protect.

The fifth signal is discomfort during handoff. If a supplier, technician, or new engineer cannot explain what matters and why, production will fill the gaps with assumptions. Those assumptions become scrap, delay, and difficult conversations later.

Another engineering loop does not need to mean a major redesign. It may mean clarifying a datum, changing a feature, documenting a setup, or running a small test against the riskiest assumption. The goal is not perfection before movement. It is enough understanding that the next stage can repeat the work without relying on luck or heroics.
