MANUFACTURING / AUG 20, 2026 / 08 MIN READ

Design for manufacturing is a conversation, not a checklist.

Design for manufacturing works best when it begins before the design feels finished.

A checklist can catch obvious problems, but it cannot replace a conversation between the people who define the product and the people who will make it. Early questions about material, tooling, assembly, inspection, and expected volume give a design room to respond while changes are still inexpensive.

That conversation should be specific. Which surfaces need to be cosmetic? Where can a tolerance open up without affecting function? Is a tight radius solving a real requirement, or is it making the tool harder to build? How will an operator orient the part, and what happens when the process drifts?

These questions are not a request to make the product less ambitious. They help the team spend ambition where it creates value. A simpler feature may improve reliability. A different joining method may remove an assembly step. A material selected with the supplier may shorten the path from prototype to repeatable production.

The strongest DFMA reviews are collaborative and early. They make tradeoffs visible, record assumptions, and leave the design team with options rather than rules. Manufacturing input is not a final approval gate. It is an engineering resource that helps the product become clearer, more robust, and more ready for the real world.
