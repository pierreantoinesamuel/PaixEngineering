PERSPECTIVE / JUL 02, 2026 / 04 MIN READ

Build the handoff
into the design.

P/E

A handoff is not a folder delivered at the end. It is the clarity that makes the next person effective.

Products pass through many hands: a designer, an analyst, a supplier, an assembler, a quality technician, and eventually the person who maintains the process. Each person brings a different context. A design that depends on the original author’s memory becomes fragile as soon as that context changes.

Build the handoff while the decisions are being made. Record why a material was selected, which dimensions protect function, where variation is acceptable, and which assumptions still need evidence. These notes do not need to become a long report. They need to be findable, specific, and connected to the work.

Drawings are important, but they are only one part of the handoff. A useful package may also include a simple assembly sequence, inspection references, test results, supplier feedback, and a short list of open risks. Together, these details help the next team understand both the product and the boundaries around it.

The best handoffs create questions at the right time instead of surprises at the wrong time. They reduce repeated explanations, protect the intent behind the design, and make improvement easier after production begins. Design for the next hand is not administrative overhead. It is one of the ways engineering creates lasting value.
